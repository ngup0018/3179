Australian SA2 Census Analysis

Exploring income distribution, rent affordability, and public service accessibility to understand demographic and regional disparities.

Population density & ambulance stations

Choropleth / centroid scatter showing median income per SA2; ambulance station locations overlayed.
Map insights
Population is highly concentrated along the eastern and southeastern coast, creating dense urban corridors. Ambulance stations cluster near those high‑density areas, while many inland SA2s remain sparsely covered. Overlaying service locations with affordability and rent‑income pressure can help prioritise resource allocation.

Income vs Rent

Scatter of median income (x) vs median rent (y). Point size encodes median age. Hover for SA2 details.
Scatter insights
The scatter shows a positive relationship between median income and median rent: higher‑income SA2s typically have higher weekly rents. Point size highlights older or younger population centres — larger points cluster towards higher rent areas. Use the age filter to reveal demographic effects on rent pressure across regions.

Affordability — Top SA2s

Ranked by rent / income ratio; cost-burdened regions highlighted.
Affordability insights
This ranking surfaces SA2s where rent consumes the largest share of personal income — key targets for housing policy. Many of the least affordable SA2s exceed the 30% rent‑to‑income threshold, indicating cost‑burdened households. Contrast these locations with population density and service access to prioritise interventions.

Income distribution

Distribution of median weekly income across SA2s (median and mean lines shown in spec).
Histogram insights
The histogram reveals the central tendency and spread of median weekly incomes across SA2s; the modal bin shows where most regions lie. Median and mean lines indicate skew; any long tails point to high‑income outliers. Use this distribution to contextualise affordability and wage comparisons across regions.
